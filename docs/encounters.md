# Points of Interest: Encounters

Twenty-one sites the King marked on his map of the western borderlands, written as full encounters. Each encounter has a matching art brief in `art/` covering the map note, the tactical map, and a portrait and token for every creature and NPC.

## Assumptions

- **Party:** 4 PCs at level 16. They will level through Chapters 9–11.
- **Threat:** measured against party level 16 unless an entry says otherwise. Four sites use level 21 creatures and wait for party level 18–19.
- **Role-play:** social encounters use the GM Core Influence subsystem.
- **Wild Hunt:** always hostile. The Zone 14 truce rule still applies, and a truce marks the PCs as future quarry.
- **Skyfall constructs:** follow the Zone 17 rule of weakness 15 to critical hits and electricity.

## Running order

Geography decides much of the order, so treat this as a pacing guide. Role-play and low-threat sites sit between the hard fights. Run #21 The Annihilator before #9 The Iron Juggernaut so the captives lead the PCs to the hauler; either order works.

## Zone 13: Tourney Fields

### 1. The Shadowless Lodge

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 13: Tourney Fields (wooded hills above the tourney fields) |
| **Hex** | 7.7 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Investigation, then combat |
| **Threat** | Low (60 XP) at party level 16 |
| **Creatures** | Ankou Assassin (level 17, *Kingmaker Adventure Path*); its Shadow Doubles appear during the fight |

**The King's map note.** A small sketch of a lodge with an empty circle drawn beside it where a shadow should fall, and the words "scouts lost — 3rd time. No shadows. Who?"

**Background.** The lodge belonged to a baron of the King's court who hosted autumn hunts here before the tournaments. The King sent three pairs of scouts to use it as a forward post. The Fey Queen's ankou now waits here to measure the PCs, and it killed the scouts to make the lodge an invitation. The ankou carves away each victim's shadow and keeps the shadows as trophies. It wants to see how the PCs think as much as how they fight. Once it has its measure, it withdraws to report to the Fey Queen.

**Arrival.**
> A lodge of dark timber sits in a clearing of bare birch, its antler-crowned gable sagging. On the threshold lies a man in royal green, face down, one hand reaching for the door. The afternoon sun lights the clearing from end to end. Every tree, stone and post casts a long shadow across the grass. The body casts none.

**Features.**
- **The threshold body.** A royal scout, dead for two days with no wounds. A DC 35 Medicine check shows the heart simply stopped. A DC 36 Occultism or Religion check identifies the missing shadow as a fey theft of the self, the signature of an assassin from the courts of the fey realm.
- **Great hall.** Double-height room with a gallery and exposed rafters 30 feet up. The rafters are greater cover for anything Large perched above, and the lodge's dimness gives concealment anywhere more than 20 feet from a window.
- **Trophy wall.** Stuffed heads of elk and boar. Five of them have their glass eyes turned to face the door. A DC 35 Perception check notices the dust on the floor below them is undisturbed; something moved them without touching the ground.
- **Scouts' bunkroom.** Five more bodies in bedrolls, all shadowless. One scout's journal records the losses in a failing hand (see Running the encounter).
- **The shadow jar.** A sealed earthenware jar on the hall's mantle. Inside, six slips of darkness flutter like moths. Opening it releases the shadows; each returns to its body and the bodies settle, at peace. A DC 36 Religion check lets a PC lay them to rest properly.

**Running the encounter.**

*Investigation (exploration mode).* Let the PCs piece together the story through these clues. Each clue found moves the scene closer to the ankou's choice to act.
- The journal: "Night three. Felgo woke screaming that something whispered his name without a voice. In the morning his shadow had gone. He died at noon."
- A DC 36 Survival check finds no tracks of the killer anywhere. A creature that flies and never lands made these kills.
- A DC 33 Nature or Occultism check recognises scratches high in the rafters as the marks of large wings folded tight.
- The ankou's telepathic whispers. Each time the party splits up, the ankou sends one isolated PC a single word: that PC's own name. Lifesense lets it track every living creature in the lodge.

*The test.* Once the PCs find the shadow jar, or after three clues, the ankou drops from the rafters behind the PC who has spoken most during the search. It opens with Shadow Doubles (spending 2 actions for two doubles) so the PCs face three ankous at once and must work out which is real. The doubles vanish when the ankou leaves or dies. The ankou targets off-guard PCs for Sneak Attack and flanks with its doubles. Its weakness to cold iron is real; a PC with a cold iron weapon becomes its priority target.

*Withdrawal.* When reduced below 150 HP, the ankou breaks off. It flies up through a gap in the roof and sends one telepathic message to every PC: "She will be pleased. You will do." It does not return. Track whether the party let it go or brought it down; encounter 14 pays this off.

**Outcomes.**
- **Ankou killed.** The PCs win a cold iron feather from its wing and an ankou-shaped silence in their dreams. Encounter 14 runs as written, with one fewer whisperer in the court.
- **Ankou escaped.** It reports to the Fey Queen, and in encounter 14 it speaks for the court and remembers the PCs by name.
- **Shadows released.** The scouts lie peacefully. Returning the bodies to their families in the King's city earns goodwill once the city is under the PCs' rule.
- **Shadows left in the jar.** The jar becomes a possible bargaining chip in encounter 14; the ankous want it back.

**Rewards.** 60 XP for the fight, plus 30 XP for releasing the shadows and identifying the killer. Treasure: the scouts' kit and pay chest (550 gp in royal coin), a *major healing potion*, and the baron's hunting arquebus from his locked gun cabinet (a *+2 greater striking greater thundering arquebus*). Kingdom: returning the scouts gains +1 Loyalty checks in the King's city for one kingdom turn after annexation.

**Scaling.** For a weaker party, skip Shadow Doubles. For party level 18, give the ankou the elite adjustment and allow Shadow Doubles twice.

### 2. The Perfect Song

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 13: Tourney Fields (high meadow above the tourney fields) |
| **Hex** | 6.8 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Role-play with an environmental hazard |
| **Threat** | Trivial (30 XP) if fought at party level 16 |
| **Creatures** | 3 cauthoojes (level 12, *Monster Core*); 4 Royal Academy composers (noncombatant NPCs) |

**The King's map note.** A single bar of music staff with five notes, the last circled three times, and "THE PERFECT SONG — Academy to transcribe. Report by Rova."

**Background.** Herders have heard a melody drifting from this meadow at dusk for years. The King, who styles himself a patron of every art, sent four composers from the Royal Academy to transcribe it for his court. The singers are three cauthoojes, flightless predator birds whose warbling song turns listeners violent and hypnotic. The composers have sat in the grass for three weeks. The song shields their minds from hunger, and they believe they are one night from finishing a masterpiece. The cauthoojes feed on the herders' goats the music draws in, and on any composer who wanders off.

**Arrival.**
> The meadow glows gold at sunset. Four people in the ruined velvet of court dress sit cross-legged in the grass, surrounded by drifts of paper weighted with stones. They are thin as rakes. All four write without pause, eyes wet, mouths moving to a tune you can almost hear. Then you hear it: a rolling, ululating song from the tall grass, beautiful enough to stop your breath.

**Features.**
- **The meadow.** Tall grass gives the cauthoojes concealment beyond 30 feet. The birds circle the composers at about 60 feet, out of sight.
- **The song (hazard).** Each dusk the birds sing for an hour. While it lasts, use the cauthoojes' Warbling Song: every creature that can hear it attempts the listed save at the start of each of its turns. A creature that spends an action humming a counter-melody (DC 35 Performance) gains a +2 circumstance bonus against it until its next turn. Stopping up the ears grants immunity but makes the PCs deaf to each other and to the composers.
- **Scores.** Hundreds of pages, half of them blank staves the composers believe they filled. Twelve pages contain a genuine, haunting composition.
- **Bones in the grass.** Goat skulls and one human hand in a velvet cuff mark the edge of the birds' feeding circle.

**Running the encounter.** The real obstacle is the composers. They refuse to leave and will defend their work, and if the PCs kill the birds before persuading them, all four collapse. The song is the only thing keeping their starving bodies going; a DC 35 Medicine check reveals that its sudden end will kill at least one of them within the hour.

*The clock.* The song lasts 6 rounds of social interaction before full dark. If the PCs have not reached 6 Influence with Maestra Rova by then, a cauthooj uses Staccato Strike to make a confused composer attack a PC, and the birds close in.

**Maestra Ilsabet Rova** (human composer, leader)
- **Perception** +18; **Will** +16
- **Discovery** DC 32 Perception, DC 30 Performance, DC 30 Academia Lore
- **Influence Skills** DC 32 Performance (finishing a phrase alongside her), DC 34 Diplomacy, DC 34 Medicine (showing her the hand in the grass and her own wasting body), DC 36 Deception
- **Influence 2:** Rova lets the PCs speak to the others and admits she has not eaten in "some days."
- **Influence 4:** Rova agrees that the work could be finished "in a proper hall." She stops writing.
- **Influence 6:** Rova orders the others to gather the scores and leave with the PCs.
- **Resistances** Threats harden her; Intimidation checks take a –2 penalty and on a failure she shields her pages with her body. Any mention of the King's deadline makes her redouble her work.
- **Weaknesses** She craves recognition. Praise of the composition backed by an actual musical demonstration grants a +2 circumstance bonus to the next Influence check. She fears the youngest composer, Pell, is dying; tending to Pell grants an extra Influence point.

*Removing the singers.* Once the composers are clear of the meadow, the birds are trivial. The PCs can fight them, scare them off with a show of force, or leave them. If the PCs fight while the composers are still present, the cauthoojes use Hop-Dodge to redirect strikes into the confused composers.

**Outcomes.**
- **Composers persuaded and fed.** All four survive. With proper food and a DC 34 Medicine check, they recover within a week.
- **Birds killed first.** The song stops; Pell dies unless a PC succeeds at a DC 36 Medicine check within 10 minutes, and the others resent the PCs.
- **PCs leave.** The composers die within days. Their scores find their way to the Academy, and the tune turns up later in a city tavern.

**Rewards.** 60 XP for reaching Influence 6 with Rova; 30 XP for the cauthoojes if fought. Treasure: the composers carry Academy stipends and court jewellery worth 900 gp, and Rova's own violin, a *moderate maestro's instrument*, which she offers in thanks. Kingdom: if the composers join the PCs' capital, they complete *The Meadow Nocturne*, a masterwork that counts as a Create a Masterpiece success for the kingdom.

**Scaling.** At party level 18, add a fourth cauthooj and raise all DCs by 3. For a shorter scene, reduce the clock to 4 rounds.

## Zone 14: Lowlands

### 3. Nine Mouths, One Belly

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 14: Lowlands (peat moor and cairnfield) |
| **Hex** | 5.7 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Role-play (negotiation by head-vote) |
| **Threat** | Trivial (30 XP) if fought at party level 16 |
| **Creatures** | Jotund troll (level 15, *Kingmaker Adventure Path*) |

**The King's map note.** A crude nine-pointed star with a stick figure inside it, the figure's head drawn separately on a pole. Beneath: "bargained with it — no."

**Background.** The King sent an envoy, Ser Halward Toll, to buy the troll's help against the PCs' kingdom. The heads could not agree on the price. The hungriest head ate Halward mid-negotiation, and his head now sits on a stake outside the cairn. Since then, one of the troll's heads talks in Halward's voice and manner; it is the troll's own head, mimicking a meal it found impressive. The troll knows the PCs outclass it. It wants something from them without dying for it, and each head wants something different.

**Arrival.**
> A barrow mound rises from the moor, its entrance stones carved with spirals. On a stake beside the entrance, a crow pecks at a head that still wears a courtier's oiled moustache. From inside the barrow comes an argument in nine voices. One of them says, in a polished court accent, "Gentlemen, please. We have guests."

**Features.**
- **The barrow.** A long chamber 30 feet high, wide enough for the troll to turn. Bones and armour carpet the floor (difficult terrain).
- **The stake.** Halward's head and his envoy's satchel, which holds the King's letter of offer (see Rewards).
- **Moor outside.** Peat bog patches act as difficult terrain; a creature that critically fails an Athletics check while running through them is immobilised until it Escapes (DC 30).

**Running the encounter.** The troll will not fight unless a head is insulted twice, the vote fails, or the PCs attack. The PCs negotiate with a council of nine heads. Five votes in favour wins the troll's agreement; five against ends the parley and starts a fight.

*The heads.* Group them so the scene stays playable. Each group votes as a bloc unless a PC wins over an individual head.
- **The Envoy** (1 head). Speaks as Halward. Vain, courtly, wants to be addressed as "Ser" and treated as a diplomat. Votes for any deal that flatters it.
- **The Bellies** (3 heads). Want food: a cow per week, a stocked hunting ground, or a meal right now. Votes with whoever feeds them.
- **The Grudges** (3 heads). Want revenge on the King for tricking them with "a meal that talked too much." They vote for any deal that hurts the King.
- **The Old Heads** (2 heads). Ancient, slow and afraid. They want the barrow left alone; it holds their mother's bones. They vote against any deal that brings humans to the moor.

**Influence (per bloc).**
- **Discovery** DC 34 Perception or Society to separate the blocs and learn each one's want.
- **Envoy:** DC 31 Diplomacy, DC 31 Society (court etiquette). Weakness: honorific titles (+2 to the check).
- **Bellies:** DC 33 Nature (offering animal husbandry or hunting rights), DC 31 Diplomacy if food is offered on the spot.
- **Grudges:** DC 34 Intimidation, DC 33 Deception or Diplomacy if the PCs share news of the King's defeat or offer them a chance to strike the King.
- **Old Heads:** DC 36 Diplomacy, DC 33 Religion (honouring the mother's bones).
- **Resistances** Any PC who speaks to only one head offends the others: –2 to the next check against a different bloc. Fire or electricity in sight (a torch, a spell) imposes a –2 penalty on all checks; trolls fear both.
- **Penalty** A critical failure flips one head of that bloc to "against."

*The deal.* Possible agreements: the troll guards the moor road for the kingdom in exchange for a cow a week; it joins the PCs' army as a mercenary against the King; it simply surrenders the satchel and lets the PCs leave in peace.

*If it fights.* The troll uses Cacophonous Roar first, then Ravenous Jaws against the nearest cluster. It flees into the moor at 120 HP, and the Old Heads berate the others as it goes.

**Outcomes.**
- **Deal struck (5+ votes).** The troll becomes a moor guardian or mercenary. It keeps its word as long as it is fed.
- **Deal fails.** Combat. The troll fights briefly, then flees.
- **Halward's head.** Returning it to his widow in the King's city reveals the King's habit of sending envoys to monsters, useful evidence in post-war politics.

**Rewards.** 60 XP for a successful deal; 30 XP if fought. Treasure: the satchel holds the King's letter, 400 gp in royal gold as the "first payment", and a *greater bottled lightning* the King sent as a sweetener. The barrow holds 1,200 gp in ancient hill-clan torcs. Kingdom: the troll as a guardian reduces the danger of one moor hex (treat the hex as having a Watchtower for Stability purposes); as a mercenary, add it to the PCs' army as a special unit at your discretion.

**Scaling.** For a tougher fight at party level 18, make the troll elite and add a second, younger jotund troll as its sibling.

### 4. The Gardeners

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 14: Lowlands (a ruined noble estate) |
| **Hex** | 8.5 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Combat with a rescue |
| **Threat** | Moderate (90 XP) at party level 16 |
| **Creatures** | 6 elite athaches (level 13, *Pathfinder Bestiary 2*) |

**The King's map note.** The outline of a manor with formal hedges, and three small upright figures drawn between them. Beneath: "Varrold estate — reclaim after the war. Statues??"

**Background.** House Varrold held this estate until the family fell from the King's favour. A family of athaches moved into the overgrown gardens and made them a larder. Their venom leaves victims enfeebled and helpless, and they stand those victims among the hedges as "statues", fed just enough water to live. The athaches call themselves gardeners and tend their collection with grotesque care. Five travellers still live, propped in the hedges.

**Arrival.**
> A manor's roofless shell stands behind formal gardens gone wild. Between the clipped hedges, figures stand on plinths like statuary: a farmer, a pedlar, a woman in a royal officer's coat. One of them turns her eyes to follow you. From the topiary maze comes the sound of shears and a voice humming, as deep as falling rock.

**Features.**
- **Topiary maze.** Hedges 15 feet high form corridors 15 feet wide. Hedges are difficult terrain to push through (DC 30 Athletics to Force Open a gap) and block line of sight. The athaches know every gap and move through them at full speed.
- **The plinths.** Five living victims, each enfeebled 3 and unable to act. An athach adjacent to a victim can use an action to grab one as a shield (the victim takes damage meant for the athach on a critical hit).
- **Compost heap.** A pit of rotting mulch and bones. A creature that falls in is slowed 1 for 1 round from the stench (DC 32 Fortitude negates).
- **Ruined manor.** Collapsed upper floor; rubble provides standard cover. The athaches throw chunks of masonry from it.

**Running the encounter.** The athaches fight as a family with roles.
- **Matriarch Gorm and her mate** hold the manor rubble and use Throw Rock from cover.
- **Four adolescents** work the maze in pairs, flanking and using fist-fist-Swift Claw combinations to apply Athach Venom.
- **Hostages.** When an athach drops below half HP, its next action grabs the nearest victim and threatens to bite. A PC can Demoralize (DC 31) or Feint to make it drop the victim. A bitten victim dies.
- **Rescue clock.** Each victim needs a DC 32 Medicine check or a cure for poison to recover enough to walk. Moving the victims out of the maze takes time; an athach that sees a victim being carried away bellows, and every athach moves to stop the theft.

**Outcomes.**
- **All athaches dead, all victims alive.** The ideal ending; the PCs gain grateful witnesses.
- **Victims lost.** Count the dead; Captain Mira Vell, the royal officer, is the most valuable survivor (below).
- **Athaches flee.** Any surviving adolescents run to the Uplands and join the six-athach band of the Zone 16 table.

**Rewards.** 90 XP; +10 XP for each living victim rescued. Treasure: the Varrold family silver and a hidden strongbox (1,600 gp total), and, among the athaches' trophies, a dead knight's *+2 greater striking halberd* and *major sturdy shield*. Kingdom: Captain Mira Vell of the royal army owes the PCs her life and can serve as an army commander or informant during the war; once the King's city falls, she can help secure the estate as a new settlement site.

**Scaling.** Use four athaches for a Low threat. At party level 18, use six elite athaches and give Gorm the elite adjustment twice (level 14).

## Zone 16: Uplands

### 5. The Ossuary Wall

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 16: Uplands (hill-fort on a high ridge) |
| **Hex** | 3.5 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Combat with a haunt |
| **Threat** | Moderate to Severe (about 110 XP) at party level 16 |
| **Creatures** | Skulltaker (level 18, *Monster Core*); the Lament of the Wall (level 15 complex haunt) |

**The King's map note.** A ring of small circles drawn like stacked skulls, with "curiosity for the Academy — 4 antiquarians sent" and later, in different ink, a single X through the ring.

**Background.** A hill-clan chieftain built this hill-fort's inner wall from the skulls of her enemies, long before the King's city existed. The bones remember. The King sent four antiquarians from the Academy to sketch and catalogue the wall; the skulltaker that coils within the ruins killed them, and Bonetaker added their skulls to the top course of the wall. The skulltaker's Skeletal Lore draws on every mind in its bones, including the antiquarians. It can speak in their voices and answer questions with their scholarship.

**Arrival.**
> A ring of grey stone crowns the ridge. The inner wall is made of skulls: thousands of them, packed tight and weathered smooth. Near the top, four are white and new, one still wearing wire spectacles. As you approach, the wind through the eye sockets rises into a moan. Then the wall speaks, in a reedy academic voice: "Do mind the third course. It's load-bearing."

**Features.**
- **Skull wall.** A 20-foot-high ring wall with three breaches. Worked stone at its base resists Splintered Ground; everywhere else in the fort is open earth and bone, where Splintered Ground works fully.
- **Inner court.** An open circle 80 feet across with a fallen standing stone at its centre (standard cover).
- **Antiquarians' camp.** Outside the wall: tents, sketchbooks, a surveyor's chain. Their notes describe the chieftain's burial chamber beneath the standing stone (the treasure cache).

**Running the encounter.**

*Parley (optional).* The skulltaker opens by talking. Through the antiquarians' voices, it offers the PCs knowledge: any Recall Knowledge check about the hill-clan history of the region or the giants of the uplands, answered with its Skeletal Lore. It wants something in return: a new skull for the wall. It suggests a prisoner, an enemy, "even one of the King's men." The PCs can extract up to three answers with DC 39 Diplomacy or Deception checks before it loses patience. Any refusal of its price ends the parley.

*The fight.* The skulltaker rises from the wall in a storm of bone and opens with Splintered Ground across the court. It keeps Shard Storm active and targets the PC who refused its bargain most loudly. It uses its fly speed to stay above melee PCs and throw bone javelins.

*The haunt.* The wall itself joins the fight.

**LAMENT OF THE WALL** — HAZARD 15
Complex, Haunt
**Stealth** +29 (expert) to notice the whispering begin before it rises
**Description** Thousands of skulls scream their old grievances in unison.
**Disable** DC 36 Religion to recite the funeral rites of the hill-clan dead (three successes required), or DC 39 Diplomacy or Hill-Clan Lore to promise the bones their chieftain will be honoured (two successes). Destroying the skulltaker also ends the haunt.
**Routine** (1 action) The wall screams. Each living creature within the ring attempts a DC 36 Will save. **Critical Success** No effect. **Success** Frightened 1. **Failure** Frightened 2, and the creature can't reduce its frightened value below 1 while inside the ring. **Critical Failure** As failure, and the creature is fleeing for 1 round.
**Reset** The haunt resets at the next dusk.

*Bonetaker threat.* Any creature that dies within 60 feet risks rising as a skeletal champion under the skulltaker's control. Keep this in mind for PC allies and companions.

**Outcomes.**
- **Skulltaker destroyed, haunt laid to rest.** The fort becomes quiet. The chieftain's chamber opens.
- **Bargain struck.** If the PCs give the skulltaker a skull, it lets them leave with their answers. Note this; a good-aligned church in the PCs' kingdom will not approve.
- **The antiquarians.** Their four skulls can be removed and returned to the Academy for burial.

**Rewards.** 110 XP. Treasure: the chieftain's chamber holds a gold torc and grave goods worth 2,400 gp, the chieftain's bronze arm-rings (*bracers of strength*), and two *major healing potions* taken from the antiquarians' packs. The antiquarians' sketchbooks are worth 300 gp to any scholar. Kingdom: returning the antiquarians' remains and notes to the Academy earns +1 to the kingdom's next Culture check after the King's city falls.

**Scaling.** Drop the haunt for a Moderate threat (80 XP). At party level 18, raise the haunt to level 17 (DCs +3) and let the skulltaker's Bonetaker raise any of the four antiquarians as skeletal champions on round 2.

### 6. The Larder

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 16: Uplands (wooded hollow below a granite shelf) |
| **Hex** | 4.4 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Combat (massacre) + rescue and evidence |
| **Threat** | Moderate 80 XP at party level 16 |
| **Creatures** | 4 hill giant butchers (level 14, *Kingmaker Bestiary*) |

**The King's map note.** A cleaver drawn beside the hollow, with "The Giant Lord's men — tolerate" underneath and a tally of six marks crossed through.

**Background.** The Giant Lord sends four butchers to stock his larder from the uplands. The King paid the Giant Lord in coin and convicts to keep the giants off the King's western roads, and the tally counts the prisoner wagons he sent. The butchers run a smokehouse camp in the hollow. Three captives still live in the cages: a royal tax clerk, a skyfall scavenger and a horse-clan outrider. The clerk carries the last delivery receipt, sealed with the King's signet.

**Arrival.**
> Woodsmoke hangs low in the hollow, thick with the smell of salt and fat. Racks of split carcasses sway from a timber frame, and behind them a row of log cages leans against the cliff. Someone in the cages starts shouting, and four huge shapes rise from the fire with axes already in hand.

**Features.**
- **Smoking racks.** Timber frames hung with carcasses. They grant standard cover and are difficult terrain. A Large creature can topple a rack with a DC 30 Athletics check; creatures in a 10-foot line beyond it take 6d6 bludgeoning damage (DC 35 basic Reflex).
- **Smokehouse.** A sod-roofed hut full of choking smoke. Creatures inside are concealed; a creature that ends its turn inside must succeed at a DC 30 Fortitude save or become sickened 1.
- **Cages.** Three log cages against the cliff. Each has Hardness 10, HP 40, and a lock (DC 30 Thievery). The butchers use the cages as hostages.
- **Fire pit.** A creature shoved or knocked prone into the pit takes 4d6 fire damage.
- **Granite shelf.** A 20-foot cliff above the hollow (DC 30 Athletics to Climb). The giants keep a rock pile on top.

**Running the encounter.** The butchers fight with confidence until two of them fall.
1. **Opening.** The first butcher to act uses **Menace Prey** on the two PCs who look richest. The others close and use **Twin Butchery** on whoever leads. One butcher climbs the shelf path and uses **Throw Rock** from the rock pile.
2. **Hostage move.** When a butcher drops below half HP, it moves to the cages and raises an axe over the clerk. It demands the PCs drop their weapons. A PC who gets to the cage first or makes a DC 32 Intimidation check stops the threat. Otherwise, on its next turn the butcher kills one captive (choose the outrider first; the clerk last).
3. **Rout.** Hill giants are cowards. Once two butchers fall, each survivor tries to flee up the shelf path toward the Giant Lord's hold. A fleeing giant who reaches the ridge warns the Giant Lord, and his forces expect the PCs.

**Twist.** The tax clerk, Ederis Pallo, recognises a PC who served at court or wore a kingdom tabard, and begs them to destroy the receipt. Ederis fears what the King's loyalists or the PCs' own court would do to him for his role. The PCs decide whether to keep the receipt as evidence of the King's deal with the giants.

**Outcomes.**
- **All giants slain.** The PCs free the captives and take the receipt. The Giant Lord loses a raiding team and learns nothing of the PCs' approach.
- **A giant escapes.** The Giant Lord's hold goes on alert; his next encounter starts with sentries ready.
- **Captives saved.** The skyfall scavenger, Hesk Varro, offers to guide the PCs to the Lightwardens (#8) or the Annihilator (#9) as payment. The horse-clan outrider owes the PCs a life-debt.
- **Receipt kept.** Proof of the King's arrangement with the Giant Lord. Presenting it in the King's city after liberation helps sway the city's nobility.

**Rewards.** 80 XP. Treasure: 1,200 gp in coin, silver plate and trade goods from the smokehouse; each butcher carries a *+2 striking wounding battle axe* and a *+1 striking returning hatchet*, sized for a Large creature (sell value or resize). The receipt grants a +2 circumstance bonus to one Kingdom check or Liberation activity tied to the city's nobility.

**Scaling.** For a stronger party, add the Giant Lord's level 16 overseer (an elite hill giant butcher). At party level 18 the encounter drops to Low; use it as a breather.

### 7. The Wyvern Queen's Hunting Ground

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 16: Uplands (collapsed ridge; also usable in Zone 17) |
| **Hex** | 1.5 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Combat, two-part (flyover, then a return grudge match) |
| **Threat** | Severe 120 XP at party level 16 |
| **Creatures** | The Wyvern Queen (level 19, *Kingmaker*) |

**The King's map note.** A crude sketch of a winged shape with a long barbed tail, scratched so hard the nib tore the vellum, with "do NOT" written beneath.

**Background.** The King once rode this ridge with a hunting party to bag a wyvern head for his palace. The Wyvern Queen killed eleven of them; he fled on foot. The ridge is her hunting ground: a collapsed shelf of shale and broken pines where she drops prey from height. A mammoth carcass hangs in the crown of a dead pine, and below it lies the half-buried shield of the King's huntmaster.

**Arrival.**
> The ridge looks broken, as though a giant fist struck it. Pines lie snapped and splintered across a slope of loose shale, and high in one dead tree hangs a mammoth, its hide stripped by talons. A shadow slides over the stones. Then the wind from her wings flattens the grass.

**Features.**
- **Shale slope.** Difficult terrain. A creature that critically fails a Reflex save or is knocked prone here slides 10 feet downslope.
- **Snapped pines.** Fallen trunks provide standard cover against ground attacks and none against a diving wyvern.
- **Hanging carcass.** The mammoth carcass is a food store and a lure. A creature that cuts it down (DC 30 Athletics or 40 slashing damage to the branch) drops it into a 15-foot burst below, dealing 8d6 bludgeoning (DC 36 basic Reflex).
- **Drop-off.** The ridge's north edge falls 80 feet into a gorge. **Punishing Momentum** drops grabbed creatures here.
- **Huntmaster's shield.** Buried in shale near the cliff. It bears the King's household crest.

**Running the encounter.**
- **Part one: the flyover.** The Wyvern Queen passes low to test the PCs. She does not attack unless attacked or unless a PC takes the mammoth carcass. If the PCs stay still and show no threat, she circles twice and leaves. If attacked, she fights from the air: **Powerful Dive** to grab a target, **Punishing Momentum** to carry them over the drop-off, **Breath Weapon** on clustered PCs, and stinger Strikes to apply **Wyvern Venom**. At 100 HP or fewer she flees to her lair (area BR3).
- **Part two: the grudge.** If she fled, she returns 1d4 days later while the PCs travel or camp. She attacks first with a **Powerful Dive** out of the sun (PCs must succeed at a DC 41 Perception check or be off-guard during the first round). She fights to the death this time and focuses the PC who wounded her most.
- **Complexity.** The PCs can lure her with the carcass and set an ambush on the ridge. A successful ambush (party chooses ground, DC 39 Survival or Nature to read her approach) lets the PCs act before her and fight her on the ground for the first round; she must spend an action to take off.

**Outcomes.**
- **Leave her alone.** She marks the PCs as uninteresting. The ridge stays dangerous for caravans.
- **Drive her off.** She returns for part two.
- **Slay her.** The ridge becomes safe; her lair at BR3 can be looted.
- **Recover the shield.** Returning the shield to the huntmaster's widow in the King's city after liberation earns goodwill among the city's old families.

**Rewards.** 120 XP (once, when she dies or leaves for good). Treasure is in her lair at BR3. Kingdom benefit: claiming the ridge hex opens a safe route through the uplands, and a wyvern trophy in the PCs' capital grants +1 Fame or an equivalent event bonus.

**Scaling.** At party level 18 she is Moderate; give her a mate (an elite wyvern) for part two. For a weaker party, run part one as a scripted flyover only.

### 8. The Lightwardens

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 17: Skyfall Wastes (hillside with a buried hull) |

| **Type** | Combat + puzzle/hazard |

**Background.** A wedge of starship hull broke off during the Skyfall and buried itself in this hillside. The radiant warden is one of its maintenance constructs. Complex, Electricity, Fire, Mechanical, Trap

**AC** 39; **Fort** +30, **Ref** +24
**Pylon Hardness** 20; **Pylon HP** 80 (BT 40); **Immunities** critical hits, object immunities, precision damage

- Its **Gatekeeper Aura** reroutes teleports back to the centre of the ring.

- **Hatch opened first.** The warden powers down. Its core can be salvaged.

### 9. The Iron Juggernaut

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 17: Skyfall Wastes (grassland scarred by an ancient patrol trench) |
| **Hex** | 0.4 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Combat + rescue against a starvation clock; linked to [#21 The Annihilator](#21-the-annihilator) |
| **Threat** | Moderate 80 XP at party level 16 |
| **Creatures** | Elite adamantine golem (level 18, Kingmaker), reflavoured as a tracked hauler |

**The King's map note.** A dotted loop drawn around a stretch of plain with an arrow showing the direction of travel. Beside it, written twice: "adamantine — HOW MUCH?" The second time is underlined. A later note in a shakier hand: "salvagers not back."

**Background.** The Juggernaut is a tracked hauler built to carry cargo to a depot that the Skyfall destroyed ten thousand years ago. Its orders never changed. It rolls its loop, reaches the crater where the depot stood, finds no one to receive its load, and turns back for another circuit. One circuit takes three days. The Juggernaut's cargo is living. Annihilators bring their captives to a docking cradle on the loop and transfer them into its hold, and the Juggernaut carries them toward a delivery that never happens. The hold has no stasis, no food and no water. Every captive delivered here starves. The floor is deep in bones, and scratched tallies cover the walls. The longest tally belongs to Tarku, a hill-clan hunter delivered a century ago, whose spear still lies with his remains. The newest tally belongs to Brann and Ottilie Kesk, two of the King's salvagers. The Annihilator caught them prying plates off the Juggernaut and delivered them nine days ago. They are still alive.

**Arrival.**
> The ground shakes before you see it. A trench cuts straight across the grassland, and along it grinds a machine the size of a house on two wide tracks, its hull plated in dark grey metal. Steam bleeds from vents along its sides. It doesn't slow as it passes. From somewhere inside comes a slow, weak knocking.

**Features.**
- **Patrol trench.** 12 feet deep and 30 feet wide, with crumbling walls (DC 30 Athletics to Climb). The Juggernaut rolls along the trench floor. Creatures on the rim have standard cover from its vents.
- **Docking cradle.** A rusted gantry of girders straddling the trench at the loop's northern bend, where the Annihilator transfers captives. A creature on the gantry can drop onto the Juggernaut's hull as it passes (DC 36 Acrobatics; on a failure, it lands prone in the trench).
- **Depot crater.** A glassed bowl at the loop's southern bend. The Juggernaut stops here for 1 minute each circuit, broadcasting a request for a receiving clerk in an antique machine dialect. This is the one time it stands still.
- **Salvage camp.** The Kesks' abandoned camp beside the trench: a toppled wagon (standard cover), pry bars, chains and a cracked plate of adamantine.
- **Cargo hold.** Reached through a dorsal hatch (two Interact actions and a DC 38 Thievery check, or DC 38 Athletics to Force Open). Inside: bones, tallies and the Kesks.

**Running the encounter.**
- **Reading the loop.** A PC who watches one full circuit, or succeeds at a DC 36 Survival check to read the trench wear, learns the route, the cradle and the stop at the depot crater. A DC 38 Crafting or Society check on the crater's broadcast reveals the Juggernaut is waiting for a clerk's authorisation code.
- **Inexorable.** The Juggernaut never leaves its route and never stops except at the crater. It uses **Inexorable March** on anything in the trench and **Vent** (described as a spray of cutting lasers) on creatures on its hull or flanks. It makes **fist** Strikes with two manipulator arms against boarders. Electricity slows it through **Golem Antimagic**; acid damages it.
- **Repair Mode.** At 0 HP the Juggernaut enters **Repair Mode** and keeps crawling at Slowed 1 while it uses **Self-Repair**. The PCs must keep it down, cut its tracks or pull its order core to end the threat for good.
- **The rescue.** The PCs don't need to destroy the Juggernaut to save the Kesks. They can board it, open the hold and carry the captives out while it rolls. A boarding party fights the Juggernaut on a moving hull: each round on the hull, a creature must succeed at a DC 32 Acrobatics or Athletics check at the start of its turn or fall prone, and a critical failure throws it into the trench.
- **Starvation clock.** The Kesks have 3 days left when the PCs first see the Juggernaut. Each day they lose 1 more point of drained. At 0 days, Brann dies first; Ottilie dies the next day.

**Outcomes.**
- **Destroyed or disabled.** The PCs reach the hold at leisure and recover the Kesks, the adamantine plating and the bones of everyone the annihilators ever delivered.
- **Boarded and rescued.** The PCs free the Kesks and leave the Juggernaut rolling. It keeps receiving the Annihilator's captives.
- **Delivery fulfilled.** A PC at the depot crater who succeeds at a DC 41 Crafting or Society check (very hard), or speaks that machine dialect and succeeds at a DC 38 Deception check, poses as the receiving clerk. The Juggernaut opens its hold, unloads, and shuts down with its orders complete. On a critical success, the PC can give it a new destination.
- **The Annihilator connection.** If the Annihilator still roams, the Juggernaut's hold fills again. Ottilie can describe the four-legged machine that caught them, and the cradle's scorch marks show where it docks.

**Rewards.** 80 XP, plus 20 XP for each Kesk rescued alive. Treasure: adamantine plating worth 3,000 gp in raw metal, the Kesks' salvage tools (a set of expanded artisan's tools), and Tarku's spear, a stone-tipped weapon wrapped in a hill-clan charm, among the bones (a *+3 greater striking spear*). Kingdom benefit: a Juggernaut with a new destination hauls skyfall salvage to the PCs' capital, worth 1 Ore commodity each kingdom turn while it runs. The Kesks will work for the kingdom out of gratitude.

**Scaling.** At party level 18, add the Annihilator docking at the cradle mid-fight. For a weaker party, remove Repair Mode.

### 10. The Scout's Wager

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 17: Skyfall Wastes (crater with a crashed escape pod) |
| **Hex** | 0.1 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Combat (hostile Wild Hunt) |
| **Threat** | Severe 120 XP at party level 16 |

**The King's map note.** A ram's horn drawn in green ink beside a small crater, and "Varga — never again. Pay them nothing."

**Background.** The King's agent, Varga Tess, once poached a white stag in the Whispering Wood and sold its antlers in the King's city. The stag was the Wild Hunt's quarry. A Wild Hunt scout named Sileth followed Varga's trail to the King's city, then to this crater, where Varga died in a crashed skyfall pod. Sileth stayed. Sileth has hung the pod with trophies and now hunts anyone carrying royal goods, coin or heraldry, as payment for the stolen kill. The PCs carry palace loot and almost certainly qualify.

**Arrival.**
> A blackened pod of strange metal lies split open at the bottom of a shallow crater. Antlers, skulls and a royal cloak hang from its ribs on lengths of green thread. Fog rolls along the crater floor, and in it you hear hounds. A voice calls from the pod, clear and amused: "You smell of the King."

- **Crater rim.** A 15-foot slope (difficult terrain going up) around the whole site.
- **Escape pod.** A split metal shell with standard cover inside and a raised perch on top. Sileth starts on the perch.
- **Trophy lines.** Green threads strung across the crater. A creature who moves through them makes them chime, alerting the hunt; they deny the benefit of Sneak or Hide to anyone crossing them.
- **Glass pools.** Pockets of fused glass in the crater floor. A creature knocked prone onto glass takes 2d6 slashing damage.
- **Fog.** The hounds' **Summon Pack** fog spreads into a 20-foot aura; Wild Hunt creatures see through it with **Greensight** and **Wild Hunt Link**.

**Running the encounter.**
- **The challenge.** Sileth calls out the terms: "The King owes the hunt a kill. You carry his gold. Run." Sileth lets the PCs take one full round to flee. The hunt is the point. The PCs may refuse to run; Sileth is delighted either way.
- **Tactics.** The hounds use **Summon Pack** on round one and **Knockdown** to drop PCs for Sileth's sneak attacks. Sileth stays mobile, firing **Befuddle** emerald beams and using **Focus Gaze** with the hounds to slow PCs inside the overlapping **Wild Gaze** auras. Sileth closes with the **Crystal Scimitar** on off-guard targets. Its wounding and keen runes make her quarry bleed (1d6 persistent bleed) and turn a hit on a natural 19 into a critical hit.
- **The wager.** A PC who throws down the royal loot and declares it forfeit (Diplomacy or Intimidation DC 41) does not end the fight. Sileth takes the offering as a sign of weakness and focuses that PC. The hunt does not bargain.
- **Truce rule.** If at least half the hunt falls (Sileth or both hounds), the survivors break off and call a truce, as the Zone 14 Wild Hunt rule states. Sileth names the PCs as future quarry and leaves a green thread tied around the wrist of the PC who fought best.
- **Weakness.** All hunt members have weakness 15 to cold iron.

**Outcomes.**
- **Sileth slain.** The hounds fight to the death. The Wild Hunt learns of it, and Sileth's death becomes a grudge the Hunt carries into Chapter 11.
- **Truce called.** Sileth leaves. The thread marks the PC; in Chapter 11 the Hunt seeks that PC first.
- **PCs flee.** The hunt pursues for one day. Repeat the encounter at a new location of your choice.

### 11. The Unmaker

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 17: Skyfall Wastes (glassed crater in scrubby hills) |
| **Hex** | 1.2 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Combat with a clock |
| **Threat** | Severe (120 XP) at party level 16 |
| **Creatures** | Guthallath (level 19, Kingmaker; robotic version with weakness 15 to critical hits and electricity); the King's engineers: Master Engineer Odalric Vane and 3 apprentices (level 8 noncombatants, use the Mechanic or Engineer statistics from NPC Core or any level 8 expert) |

**The King's map note.** A circle hatched black, with a stick figure the size of a tower standing inside it. Beside it: "the Colossus — wake it for the war?" A second line, in a steadier hand: "Vane says three days. Pay him double."

**Background.** Ages ago a starship fragment gouged this crater, and a giant war construct came with it. It has stood half-buried in fused glass ever since, dormant. The King hired Odalric Vane, a disgraced salvage-town technician, to wake it as a weapon against the PCs' kingdom. The King fell before Vane finished. Vane kept working anyway; he has spent the King's gold and has nowhere to go, and the Colossus is the only thing he has left. His control sigils give partial command of the construct, and they fail the moment it fully wakes. Once awake, the guthallath obeys nobody.

**Arrival.**
> The crater floor gleams like a frozen lake of green glass. At its heart a figure the height of a watchtower stands buried to the waist, its chest plates daubed with chalk symbols. Sparks jump between copper cables strung from its shoulders to a ring of humming iron drums, and four small figures scurry along a scaffold at its collar. A haze of glittering dust hangs around the giant like breath on a winter morning.

**Features.**
- **Glass floor.** The crater floor counts as uneven ground (DC 30 Acrobatics to Balance when Striding at full speed). A creature knocked prone slides 10 feet downslope toward the centre.
- **Scaffold.** Timber scaffolding rises 60 feet up the guthallath's front. Climbing it takes a DC 25 Athletics check. The guthallath smashes the scaffold on its first turn awake; anyone on it falls unless they Grab an Edge.
- **Capacitor drums.** Six iron drums in a ring 80 feet from the centre feed the waking ritual. Each has AC 30, Hardness 10, 40 HP. A destroyed drum discharges 6d6 electricity in a 10-foot burst (DC 36 basic Reflex). The guthallath has weakness 15 to electricity, so a drum blast that catches it deals the extra damage.
- **Nanite haze.** The guthallath's Erosion Aura manifests as clouds of deconstructor nanites. They also erode the scaffold and the drums once it wakes.
- **Vane's wagon.** A covered wagon at the crater's rim holds Vane's notes, tools and the last of the King's gold.

**Running the encounter.**

*The Waking Clock.* The clock starts at 0 and fills at 6. It advances by 1 at the end of each round while at least three capacitor drums still stand and Vane or an apprentice tends the sigils. At 6 the guthallath wakes fully. The PCs can:
- Persuade Vane to stop. Use the Influence block below. Each round spent talking still advances the clock unless an apprentice is also stopped.
- Destroy capacitor drums. With two or fewer standing, the clock stops.
- Scrub the sigils. Arcana, Crafting or Occultism DC 38 (two actions, adjacent to the chest) removes 1 from the clock. A critical success removes 2.
- Attack the guthallath while it sleeps. Damage to it advances the clock by 1 per hit as the defence systems spark awake.

*Half-awake (clock 3–5).* The guthallath can't move its feet from the glass. It acts once per round with two actions only. It uses Fist Strikes and Improved Grab against anyone on the scaffold, and Deadly Throw to fling climbers over the crater's rim.

*Awake (clock 6).* The guthallath tears free, smashes the scaffold, and fights at full strength. It opens with Annihilation Beams down the longest line of PCs, then wades in with Trample and Fist. It uses Improved Push to shove PCs into the remaining capacitor drums. Vane screams that he can still control it; he can't. The guthallath kills the engineers first if the PCs give it nobody better to fight.

*Influence: Odalric Vane* (level 8 human engineer)
- **Discovery** DC 26 Perception or Society; DC 24 Crafting
- **Influence Skills** DC 28 Crafting (talking shop and pointing out the sigils' flaw), DC 26 Diplomacy, DC 30 Intimidation (he fears the PCs less than he fears poverty), DC 26 Society (offering a post in the PCs' kingdom)
- **Influence 2** Vane admits the sigils fail once the Colossus wakes. He orders one apprentice off the scaffold; the clock advances only every other round.
- **Influence 4** Vane stops work. The apprentices climb down, and the clock stops. He offers to help shut the construct down: with his help, scrubbing the sigils takes DC 34.
- **Resistances** Mention of the King's defeat hardens him (+2 DC on the next check) unless the PC also offers him a future.
- **Weaknesses** Praise for his craft or an offer of employment (–2 DC).
- **Penalty** On a critical failure, Vane triggers the final sigil out of spite; the clock jumps by 2.

**Outcomes.**
- **Guthallath never wakes.** The PCs win without a full fight, but the Colossus remains a dormant hazard. Kingdom engineers can study it for generations.
- **Guthallath destroyed.** Its core yields salvage (see Rewards). Vane, if alive, weeps over it and then asks for a job.
- **Vane hired.** He becomes a kingdom NPC who grants +1 circumstance bonus to Engineering activities for one kingdom turn per kingdom level advance, at your discretion.
- **Vane dead.** His notes reveal the King's plan to march the Colossus on the PCs' capital.

**Rewards.** 120 XP for a fight; award 80 XP if the PCs stop the waking without fighting. Treasure: 2,000 gp in royal coin in Vane's wagon; the guthallath's core, a chunk of standard-grade adamantine and skymetal circuitry worth 3,500 gp to a smith or to skyfall traders; Vane's notebook (the formula for a *siege drill (Mark III)*, a level 16 item). Kingdom: salvaging the core grants 1 Commodity of Ore and Luxuries each.

**Scaling.** For a weaker party, end the encounter at clock 5 with a half-awake guthallath that never tears free. At party level 18 or above, start the clock at 2 and let the guthallath wake with all six drums intact.

### 12. The Storm Tree

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 18: Whispering Wood (forest edge, lightning-scorched clearing) |

| **Type** | Combat and exploration |

**The King's map note.** A tree sketched with legs in place of roots and a jagged lightning bolt through the trunk. Underneath: "the tree that moves." Beside it, a tally of eleven marks and the words "Brannock's camp. All."

**Background.** One of the elder fey woke this sard to guard the threshold of the Whispering Wood. It treats every axe as a declaration of war. The King sent foreman Brannock and ten woodcutters to log the forest edge for warship timber. The sard killed them all in one night. The logging camp still stands half-burned, and the sard has rooted itself at its centre to watch the treeline. Electricity crawls through the soil around it. Brannock's ledger, sealed in a strongbox, records that the woodcutters heard a voice in the thunder ask them to stop, three times, before the sard moved.

**Arrival.**
> Charred stumps stand in neat rows where a forest was felled. Beyond them, half a logging camp still smoulders: collapsed tents, a sawpit, a cart heaped with split timber. At the camp's heart stands a black tree twice the height of any other, its bark split by veins of red-white light. Thunder grumbles from a cloudless sky. The tree's roots shift.

**Features.**
- **Stump field.** The felled rows count as difficult terrain. Each stump provides lesser cover to a Medium creature.
- **Charged ground.** The soil within 30 feet of the sard holds a static charge. A creature that starts its turn there in contact with the ground takes 2d6 electricity damage.
- **Timber cart.** A heaped cart of split logs. A PC can Shove it downhill (DC 30 Athletics) into a 20-foot line, dealing 6d10 bludgeoning (DC 36 basic Reflex) to creatures in its path. It works once.
- **Sawpit.** A 10-foot-deep pit. The sard can Trample PCs into it; a creature knocked in lands prone.
- **Strongbox.** Iron, half-buried beside the foreman's tent, 40 feet from the sard. Unlocking it takes a DC 34 Thievery check; prying it open takes a DC 36 Athletics check.
- **Thunderhead.** The sky darkens as the fight goes on. From round 3, lightning strikes the tallest metal object in the clearing each round (6d6 electricity, DC 36 basic Reflex).

**Running the encounter.**

*Warning.* The sard speaks through the thunder in Sylvan before it moves. It asks intruders who carry axes, saws or royal tools to leave. A PC who answers in Sylvan and lays down tools can attempt a DC 41 Diplomacy or Nature check to Request safe passage across the clearing. On a success, the sard lets the party cross once and fights only if attacked. On a failure, it attacks.

*Tactics.* The sard opens with Thorn Volley against up to four targets, holding the treeline. It then Strides in and uses Trample to drive PCs through the charged ground and into the sawpit. It uses Trunk and Root Strikes on whoever deals the most damage. Its Lightning-Struck Curse punishes PCs who rely on electricity resistance. It fights to the death. When it dies, Splintering Death explodes in a 30-foot emanation; a successful DC 34 Nature check warns the PCs a round early as the bark begins to split.

*Exploration layer.* The strongbox holds Brannock's ledger. Recovering it during the fight means darting into the charged ground near the sard. The ledger proves the sard asked the woodcutters to stop three times. That knowledge matters for the kingdom's later relationship with the Whispering Wood.

**Outcomes.**
- **Sard slain.** The forest edge opens. Lumber camps can be built here, but the elder fey notice; the Fey Queen's later attentions grow sharper.
- **Passage granted.** The PCs cross and keep the sard as a neutral threshold guardian. A later visit carrying gifts of seed rather than steel could earn its help.
- **Ledger recovered.** The PCs learn that the Whispering Wood warns before it kills. Grant +2 circumstance bonus to the first Diplomacy or Nature check with a fey of the Whispering Wood.

**Rewards.** 120 XP. Treasure: Brannock's strongbox holds 1,800 gp of wages; a cold iron greataxe (high-grade cold iron) lies in the foreman's tent; a heartwood shard from the slain sard works as a level 17 magic item component worth 3,000 gp. Kingdom: claiming the cleared hex adds a lumber camp site without the usual Wilderness penalty.

**Scaling.** For an easier fight, remove the Thunderhead and the charged ground. At party level 18, add the Thunderhead from round 1.

### 13. The Quilled Hunter

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 18: Whispering Wood (a winding canyon under the forest canopy) |
| **Hex** | 5.2 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Exploration chase, then combat |
| **Threat** | Severe (120 XP) at party level 16 |
| **Creatures** | Primal bandersnatch (level 19, Kingmaker) |

**The King's map note.** A canyon drawn as a long crooked line, with a cat sketched at its end: six legs, quills like a hedgehog. Above it: "Wren's bow is here. Wren isn't." A row of small crosses marks the canyon walls.

**Background.** The bandersnatch hunts only clever prey and keeps trophies from the cleverest. Its den lies at the far end of a narrow canyon roofed by forest. The canyon walls hold its trophies: a manticore's tail, a troll king's crown, a whimwyrm's skull, and the bow of Wren Ashby, the King's finest ranger. The King sent Wren to track the beast and bring back a pelt for his throne room. Wren lasted four days. The bandersnatch has scented the PCs before they enter the canyon, and it has decided they are worth hunting.

**Arrival.**
> The canyon narrows until the canopy closes overhead and the light turns green. Pale objects hang from the walls on vines: a jawbone, a cracked crown, a spiked tail longer than a horse. Something heavy moved through here recently. A long gouge of quills scars the moss, and the tracks show six feet.

**Features.**
- **The Canyon.** Two miles long, 30 to 80 feet wide. Walls rise 60 feet and climb at DC 30 Athletics.
- **Trophy walls.** Each trophy hangs on a vine net. Cutting one down makes noise (see the Hunt Clock).
- **Thorn thickets.** Dense patches of thorny brush count as greater difficult terrain and provide concealment.
- **Den.** A sandy hollow under a fallen tree at the canyon's end, strewn with bones. Wren's bow and pack lie here.

**Running the encounter.**

*The Hunt Clock.* Run the canyon as a chase in reverse. The bandersnatch stalks the PCs through the canyon over five stages: Entrance, Trophy Walls, Thicket Narrows, Broken Bridge, Den. Each stage requires one of the listed checks from each PC. The **Hunt Clock** starts at 0. Each failed check adds 1; each critical failure adds 2. At 6, the bandersnatch strikes.

| Stage | Obstacle | Checks |
|---|---|---|
| Entrance | Track it before it tracks you | DC 39 Survival or Perception; DC 41 Nature |
| Trophy Walls | Read the trophies for its habits | DC 39 Nature or Society; DC 36 Occultism |
| Thicket Narrows | Pass the thorns quietly | DC 39 Stealth; DC 41 Athletics |
| Broken Bridge | Cross a log bridge over a crevasse | DC 36 Acrobatics; DC 39 Athletics |
| Den | Search the den before it returns | DC 36 Perception; DC 39 Survival |

A PC who succeeds at the Trophy Walls check learns that the bandersnatch has weakness to cold iron and that its gaze confuses. A success at the Den recovers Wren's bow and pack.

*Ambush.* If the Hunt Clock reaches 6, the bandersnatch attacks from above with Frumious Charge, and the PCs are off-guard on the first round. If the PCs reach the Den before 6, they choose the ground and the bandersnatch arrives in round 1 normally; they may set one prepared trap or ambush of your approval.

*Tactics.* The bandersnatch opens with Frumious Charge, then uses Focus Gaze to confuse the strongest melee PC. It uses Lash Out and its tail Attack of Opportunity to punish movement. Its quills cause Pain (drained). Fast Healing and Quick Recovery let it shrug off conditions, so it withdraws into the thickets when below 100 HP to heal, then returns. It fights to the death once in its den.

**Outcomes.**
- **Bandersnatch slain.** The PCs claim its trophies and its pelt. Its quills are prized by fletchers.
- **Bandersnatch escapes.** It hunts the PCs again on their next trip into the Whispering Wood, starting the Hunt Clock at 3.
- **Wren's pack recovered.** It holds Wren's journal, which maps two hidden trails in the Whispering Wood and records a meeting with a raven-masked fey (foreshadowing #16).

**Rewards.** 120 XP; award an extra 30 XP if the PCs reach the Den without triggering the ambush. Treasure: Wren's bow is a *+2 greater striking speed composite longbow*; the troll king's crown is worth 1,500 gp; the bandersnatch pelt and quills are worth 2,500 gp. Kingdom: a bandersnatch pelt in the throne room grants +1 Fame once.

**Scaling.** For a weaker party, give a +2 circumstance bonus to the stage checks. At party level 18, the bandersnatch arrives at Hunt Clock 5.

### 14. The Shadow Court

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 18: Whispering Wood (a grove of dead trees deep in the forest) |
| **Hex** | 7.0 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Role-play (audience) |
| **Threat** | Severe (140 XP) at party level 16 if it becomes a fight |
| **Creatures** | Ankou assassin (level 17, Kingmaker), called the Speaker; 4 ankous (level 14, Bestiary 2) |

**The King's map note.** A ring of five dead trees drawn with grey cloaks hung from each branch. One cloak is coloured in solid black. Beneath, in a shaky hand: "they watched me. they let me leave." The note has no date.

**Background.** This grove is where the Fey Queen's ankous gather between tasks. The PCs met an ankou at the Shadowless Lodge (#1), and this is that thread's payoff. The ankous have tested the PCs and found them worthy of a closer look. The Speaker summoned them here to take their measure for the Fey Queen, the way a courtier inspects a gift before it reaches the throne. The ankous want information: what the PCs value, what they fear, and how they will rule. In return, they will answer some questions. Every answer they give, and every answer they get, reaches the Fey Queen. If the PCs spared an ankou at the Shadowless Lodge, that ankou is here and remembers.

**Arrival.**
> The trees here have been dead for a century, yet none has fallen. Grey cloaks hang from their branches like sleeping bats. As you step into the grove, the cloaks unfold. Five figures drop to the ground without a sound and settle in a ring around you, wings folded, eyeless faces turned your way. A voice speaks inside your head, dry as paper. "Sit. The Court will hear you."

**Features.**
- **The ring.** Five dead trees around a clearing of grey moss. A stone table stands at its centre, laid with an untouched place setting for one.
- **The empty chair.** A carved chair at the table's head, draped in black silk. Nobody sits in it. A PC who studies it (DC 38 Occultism or Religion) senses that it is kept ready for someone powerful who watches through it.
- **Dead canopy.** Dim light throughout; creatures in the grove have concealment from anyone outside it.
- **Hanging cloaks.** Dozens of empty ankou cloaks hang in the branches. Each marks a kill. One bears the badge of a royal scout.

**Running the encounter.**

*Structure.* The audience runs as an exchange. The Speaker asks a question; a PC answers; then the PCs may ask one question in return. The Court runs for five exchanges. Track the Court's **Regard** with the Influence block below. The ankous speak only through Telepathy.

*The Court's questions.* Pick or adapt:
1. "What would you burn to keep your crown?"
2. "Name the person in your realm you trust least."
3. "If the forest asked for your kingdom, what would you ask in return?"
4. "Which of you is the ruler, truly?"
5. "What do you dream of on the longest night?"

Honest answers raise Regard. Lies raise it too if the liar succeeds at a DC 39 Deception check; the Court admires skilled liars. Every answer, true or false, reaches the Fey Queen. Record what the PCs say: you can use it in Chapters 10 and 11.

*Influence: the Speaker* (ankou assassin, level 17)
- **Discovery** DC 36 Perception; DC 34 Occultism or Nature (fey lore)
- **Influence Skills** DC 38 Deception, DC 38 Diplomacy, DC 41 Intimidation, DC 36 Performance (the Court enjoys a well-told story), DC 36 Nature or Occultism (proper courtly etiquette of the fey realm)
- **Influence 3** The Court answers one question about the Fey Queen truthfully: her name, that she rules from the House at the Edge of Time, or that she was once one of the elder fey's favourites.
- **Influence 5** The Speaker reveals that the Fey Queen intends to absorb the PCs' kingdom into the fey realm once it is grand enough.
- **Influence 7** The Speaker gives the PCs a black feather. Showing it once to any ankou prevents that ankou from attacking for one encounter.
- **Resistances** Threats provoke contempt (+2 DC on Intimidation). Mentioning the Trickster Lord makes the Speaker cold (+5 DC on the next check).
- **Weaknesses** Answering a question with a story or a song (–2 DC on Performance). Showing a cloak or token taken from a spared ankou (–2 DC on all checks).
- **Penalty** Attacking an ankou, refusing to answer twice, or failing two checks by 10 or more ends the audience. The Court attacks.

*If it becomes a fight.* The Speaker uses Shadow Doubles for three actions at once, flooding the grove with duplicates. The four ankous fly in pairs, flanking for Sneak Attack. They fight to drive the PCs from the grove and withdraw if the Speaker dies.

**Outcomes.**
- **Audience completed.** The PCs learn about the Fey Queen early, and she learns about them. Use their answers in Chapter 10.
- **Feather earned.** One ankou encounter later in the campaign becomes a parley.
- **Fight.** The Court scatters. The Fey Queen's agents treat the PCs as enemies from then on; raise the DC of later fey social checks by 2.

**Rewards.** 140 XP for a full audience (Influence 5 or higher) or a fight; 80 XP for a partial one. Treasure: the royal scout's cloak hides a waxed packet holding 1,200 gp in gems and a *scroll of regenerate*; the black feather (above). Kingdom: none, but the knowledge prepares the PCs for Chapter 10.

**Scaling.** For a weaker party, drop two ankous. At party level 18, add a second ankou assassin as the Speaker's second.

### 15. The Moody Brood

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 18: Whispering Wood (a grove where the seasons change) |
| **Hex** | 5.0 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Role-play (whims) |
| **Threat** | Severe (120 XP) at party level 16 if it becomes a fight |
| **Creatures** | 2 whimwyrms (level 17, Kingmaker): Pippet and Gloamsy |

**The King's map note.** A circle divided into four wedges like a sundial, each coloured for a season: green, gold, rust, white. Two tiny curled dragons perch on the rim. Underneath: "charming!! bring the court." Below that, struck through twice: "never again."

**Background.** Two whimwyrm siblings play in a grove where the seasons turn every few minutes. They are bored, vain and lethal. The King once brought twelve courtiers here for a picnic. The wyrms enjoyed the court's songs for an hour, then took offence at a joke and ate the jester. The King fled with seven courtiers. The siblings remember him fondly as "the funny king who ran." They test every visitor with whims: small demands that change with their moods. Satisfy enough whims, and they reward the PCs. Insult them, and they fight.

**Arrival.**
> Spring blossom falls around you, then turns to golden leaves before it lands. Snow dusts the leaves a minute later. In the middle of the clearing two sleek dragons, no longer than a person, lounge on a mossy stone. One is the colour of dawn, the other of dusk. The dawn-coloured one yawns. "Oh, good. Visitors. Are you funny?"

**Features.**
- **Turning seasons.** At the start of each round (or every minute out of combat), the season changes in order: spring, summer, autumn, winter. Spring: the ground counts as difficult terrain (mud). Summer: no effect. Autumn: falling leaves grant concealment. Winter: snow counts as difficult terrain and creatures without cold resistance take 2d6 cold at the end of each turn.
- **Mossy stone.** The wyrms' throne; standing on it uninvited is an insult.
- **Picnic remains.** A ruined banquet table from the King's visit, with a jester's cap and a broken lute.

**Running the encounter.**

*Whims.* Each wyrm has a **Mood** track from 0 (sulking) to 10 (delighted); both start at 5. Each exchange, one wyrm makes a whim. A PC answers with the listed skill. Success raises that wyrm's Mood by 1; critical success by 2; failure lowers it by 1; critical failure by 2. Whims change mid-request about half the time; when the wyrm changes its mind, the PC must attempt the new check with no time to prepare.

| d6 | Whim | Check |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Tell us a joke. A new one." | DC 38 Performance or Deception |
| 2 | "Fetch the snow from the summer side." | DC 36 Athletics or Acrobatics, or a relevant spell |
| 3 | "Compliment my sister. Now insult her. Better than that." | DC 38 Diplomacy, then DC 38 Performance |
| 4 | "Riddle me." | DC 36 Society or Occultism to pose a riddle they can't solve |
| 5 | "Dance until the season changes." | DC 36 Performance or Acrobatics |
| 6 | "Give me something shiny." | Offer an item worth 100 gp or more; no check, +1 Mood |

*Pippet* (dawn-coloured) loves flattery and hates being ignored. Raise the DC by 2 if a PC addresses Gloamsy first. *Gloamsy* (dusk-coloured) loves dark jokes and hates being touched. A Strike, touch spell or uninvited Interact against Gloamsy sets her Mood to 0.

*Outcomes of the Mood track.*
- **Either wyrm at 10.** That wyrm grants a gift (see Rewards).
- **Both wyrms at 8 or higher after six whims.** The siblings tell the PCs a secret: where the Trickster Lord's emissary roosts (see #16), or which hex holds the Fey Queen's next ankou ambush.
- **Either wyrm at 0.** Combat starts. Use the whimwyrm's Insult Challenges ability: if a PC was insulting the wyrm when combat began, they roll Performance for initiative.

*If it becomes a fight.* The wyrms open with Mind-Numbing Breath in overlapping cones, then use Frenzied Fangs and Whiplashing Tail with Meddling Tail to trip PCs into the snow. They fly above the canopy when below half HP and may return later in a better mood.

**Outcomes.**
- **Delighted wyrms.** The PCs make two fickle friends. Once per book, the wyrms may appear unbidden to help or hinder, rolling a d6: on 1–3, they help.
- **Sulking wyrms.** The PCs leave unmolested but unrewarded.
- **Combat.** The wyrms remember. Every later encounter with whimwyrms starts with both at Mood 2.

**Rewards.** 120 XP for a fight or for reaching Mood 10 with both wyrms; 60 XP for a peaceful partial success. Treasure: Pippet's gift is a mantle of rose-gold scales, a *major unmemorable mantle*; Gloamsy's gift is a jester's cap from the King's court, now enchanted as a *troubadour's cap*. The picnic remains hold the King's silver service, worth 1,000 gp. Kingdom: hosting the wyrms at a kingdom festival grants +1 Fame once and a story your bards will tell for decades.

**Scaling.** For a weaker party, lower all whim DCs by 2. At party level 18, add a third sibling, Hullabalo, who wants whatever the other two don't.

### 16. The Trickster's Emissary

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 18: Whispering Wood (old-growth forest, a moss-floored clearing ringed by standing stones) |
| **Hex** | 8.1 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Role-play (verbal fencing); combat only if the PCs force it |
| **Threat** | Extreme 160 at party level 16 if fought. The emissary pulls its punches, so a fight plays closer to Severe. |
| **Creatures** | Vilderavn herald (L19, Kingmaker) riding a zomok (L16, Bestiary 2) |

**The King's map note.** A raven drawn in black ink with a single eye left blank, and beneath it: "the raven. it knew my name before I spoke. DO NOT RETURN."

**Background.** The vilderavn serves the Trickster Lord. Its master sent it to watch the Fey Queen's plan ripen: she must absorb a thriving mortal kingdom to complete her apology, so the Trickster Lord wants the PCs' kingdom to flourish. The herald poses as the Fey Queen's warden and orders intruders out of the Whispering Wood. Years ago it landed in front of the King on one of his hunting progresses and gave him the same speech. He fled and never came back. The herald remembers him with contempt and the PCs with interest. This encounter happens only before Chapter 10. After Chapter 10 begins, replace it with a Wild Hunt patrol (2 archers and 4 hounds, as Zone 18).

**Arrival.**
> The birdsong stops all at once. A shape the size of a cottage drops through the canopy on leaf-green wings, and a dragon of root and bark settles into the clearing, its breath smelling of turned soil. On its back sits a knight in black plate whose helm is shaped like a raven's skull. "You walk in the queen's wood," the knight says. "Turn around. Your realm needs its rulers more than this forest needs your boots."

**Features.**
- **Standing stones.** Seven moss-covered stones ring the clearing and grant standard cover. The herald lands at the centre.
- **Old-growth trunks.** Trees outside the ring count as forest terrain for the zomok's Forest Step and provide greater cover to anyone who hides among them.
- **Leaf litter.** The clearing floor is soft but even. Beyond the stones, roots make the ground difficult terrain.
- **The ninth stone.** A fallen stone lies outside the ring. Carved faces on it (Society or Occultism DC 36 to Recall Knowledge) show a lantern-bearing figure the old hill clans called the Trickster Lord. The carving lets the PCs name the herald's real master.

**Running the encounter.** The herald opens with a demand and keeps the conversation on its terms. Run it as an Influence encounter. The PCs have 4 rounds of conversation before the herald loses patience and orders them to leave. If they refuse at that point, it attacks.

**Vilderavn herald (Influence)**
- **Perception** DC 39 (Sense Motive against its lies)
- **Discovery** DC 37 Fey Lore or Occultism; DC 39 Perception, Nature or Society
- **Influence Skills** DC 39 Deception (playing along with the lie), DC 39 Diplomacy, DC 41 Intimidation, DC 37 Performance (it admires a well-turned courtly phrase), DC 36 Fey Lore or Lore about the elder fey
- **Influence 2:** The herald concedes that the PCs may pass along the forest's edge unharmed. It lets slip that it "serves a crown older than any queen."
- **Influence 4:** The herald admits it serves the Trickster Lord. It hints that the Trickster Lord wants the PCs' kingdom strong and that "a gift grown fat is a gift worth giving." Perceptive PCs (DC 39 Perception or Sense Motive) realise the Fey Queen means to take their kingdom.
- **Influence 6:** The herald, amused, offers a token: a black raven feather. Once, a creature holding it may ask the Trickster Lord's court a single question in Chapter 11, or it may serve as safe passage past one fey guardian loyal to the Trickster Lord (GM's choice). It then departs.
- **Resistances** Threats over the PCs' kingdom's strength amuse the herald (+2 DC to Intimidation). It hates being called the Fey Queen's servant once its cover breaks (+2 DC to Diplomacy that round).
- **Weaknesses** Courtly etiquette and flattery of the Trickster Lord (–2 DC to Diplomacy and Performance). Mentioning the King's flight lets the herald sneer at him at length, and it reveals one discovery for free.
- **Penalty** Each failure on an Influence check costs the PCs 1 round. Critical failures with Intimidation trigger combat.

**Combat.** If combat begins, the herald fights to drive the PCs out and avoids killing them. It uses Aura of Disquietude to break the party's cohesion and its greatsword for nonlethal attacks once a PC drops below a quarter of their HP. The greatsword carries greater brilliant and greater fearsome runes: each hit deals an extra 1d4 fire, and a critical hit leaves the target frightened 2 and blinded for 1 round unless it succeeds at a DC 41 Fortitude save. The zomok uses Entombing Breath on clustered PCs and Swallow Whole on the strongest melee combatant. It spits the creature out at the forest edge when the fight ends. Both withdraw with Forest Step if either drops below 100 HP, and the herald calls out: "Go home. Grow strong. Someone is counting on you."

**Outcomes.**
- **Influence 6 or more.** The PCs learn that the Trickster Lord backs their kingdom's growth for his own ends and gain the raven feather.
- **Influence 2–5.** The PCs learn some of the truth and may pass.
- **Influence 0–1 and the PCs leave.** The herald watches them go. They may return later.
- **Combat.** The emissary withdraws and never appears again in Zone 18. The PCs keep the clue from the ninth stone only.
- **Herald slain.** A rare outcome. The Trickster Lord notices. In Chapter 11, the court treats the PCs with hostility from the first scene (+2 DC to social checks with Trickster Lord loyalists).

**Rewards.** 160 XP for an Influence 6 resolution or for driving the pair off. Treasure: the raven feather (unique, story item). The herald's plate holds no coin. If the herald dies, its black full plate is *+2 greater resilient full plate* (level 14) and the zomok's heartwood is a 3,000 gp crafting reagent for a primal item.

**Scaling.** For a weaker party, raise the patience to 6 rounds and lower DCs by 2. For a stronger party, have the herald dismount and send the zomok after anyone who wanders off.

### 17. Spore-Dawn

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 18: Whispering Wood (a sinkhole hollow in the forest's heart) |
| **Hex** | 7.2 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Combat set piece with an objective |
| **Threat** | Beyond Extreme at party level 16; hold this site. Severe 120 at party level 18, Moderate 80 at party level 19. |
| **Creatures** | Mu spore (L21, Kingmaker) |

**The King's map note.** A circle shaded with tiny dots, an arrow pointing down into it, and the words "omen — of what?" in the court astrologer's careful hand. The King added underneath in his own scrawl: "burn the astrologer."

**Background.** Mu spores appear over places on the brink of catastrophe. The Fey Queen's growing hold on the borderlands has drawn one. It has anchored itself above a sinkhole and has started seeding the trees with burrowing spores. In a month the corrupted trees will spread spores on the wind toward the PCs' nearest settlement. The spore has no master; it feeds on the doom it senses. The King's astrologer charted its drift for a season and left a notebook in a hunting blind on the hollow's rim.

**Arrival.**
> The forest goes grey. Bark peels from the trees in pale sheets, and every trunk weeps a fine dust that hangs in the air like smoke. Above the hollow ahead, blotting out half the sky, hangs a shape of flesh and fungus a hundred feet across, its tentacles trailing into the trees below. It breathes, and the whole forest leans toward it.

**Features.**
- **Spore-rotted trees.** Twenty trees around the hollow are corrupted. Each has AC 25, Hardness 5 and 60 HP. While three or more corrupted trees stand, the mu spore's Spores aura DC rises by 1 and it regains 10 additional HP from regeneration each round.
- **The sinkhole.** The hollow drops 60 feet to a spore-carpeted floor. The spore hovers 40 feet above the rim. Climbing the sinkhole walls is a DC 36 Athletics check.
- **The astrologer's blind.** A collapsed wooden blind on the rim holds the notebook and the astrologer's corpse, overgrown with mould.
- **Sound.** The mu spore's regeneration stops when sonic damage hits it. The astrologer's notebook records that "thunder made it shudder", a clue PCs can find with a DC 38 Society or Occultism check while they read it.

**Running the encounter.** The fight has two goals: kill the mu spore and destroy enough corrupted trees to stop the spread. Run a 6-round clock from the moment the spore notices the PCs. At the end of round 6, any surviving corrupted trees release spores on the wind, and the nearest PC settlement suffers a kingdom event (treat as Plague). Killing the mu spore ends the clock at once.

Tactics: the mu spore opens with Enormous Inhalation to pull the party into its 60-foot Spores aura, then uses Cough Spores on anyone who stays outside. It grabs anyone in melee with Grasping Tendrils and uses Fast Swallow on the first creature it grabs with its jaws. It uses its fly speed to stay above the sinkhole, which forces melee PCs to fly or climb. It fights to the death; it has no concept of retreat.

**Outcomes.**
- **Spore slain, trees cleared.** The forest heals within the season. The PCs' kingdom gains 1 Fame for ending a known omen of doom.
- **Spore slain, trees standing.** The spread still happens at the end of the clock, though at half strength (resolve the Plague event with a +2 circumstance bonus).
- **PCs retreat.** The spore stays. The clock keeps running in weeks instead of rounds; each week the PCs delay adds another corrupted tree.

**Rewards.** 120 XP at party level 18. Treasure: the astrologer's satchel holds 2,500 gp in royal gold and a *major healing potion*. The notebook names three more omens the astrologer charted (use as adventure hooks). The mu spore's core sac, harvested with a DC 40 Crafting or Nature check, is worth 4,000 gp as an alchemical reagent.

**Scaling.** At party level 19, add 10 more corrupted trees. To run it earlier, reduce the mu spore to a weak adjustment (L20) and shorten the clock to 4 rounds; the encounter becomes Extreme at party level 16, so warn the players with the omen.

### 18. The Linnorm's Pass

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 19: Western Mountains (a high pass with an avalanche chute and a lava vent) |
| **Hex** | 3.0 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Role-play (bargain); combat at party level 18 or later |
| **Threat** | Bargain at any level. Fight: beyond Extreme at 16, Severe 120 at party level 18, Moderate 80 at party level 19. |
| **Creatures** | Tor linnorm (L21, Monster Core) |

**The King's map note.** A dashed line labelled "trade road west" stops dead at a coiled serpent drawn in red ink. The note reads: "blocked — serpent. Sent 40 men. Sent 12 more. No."

**Background.** The tor linnorm Vashkra has lain in this pass for four centuries. She sleeps on a magma vent that keeps her coils warm, and she counts the pass as part of her body. The King wanted a western trade route over the Western Mountains and sent soldiers twice; their bones line the chute. Vashkra wants two things: a tribute worthy of her age, and news of the world beyond the mountains, which she has not left in a century. Linnorms curse their killers, and Vashkra knows it. She enjoys reminding anyone who threatens her.

**Arrival.**
> Steam rolls down the pass from a crack in the mountainside where orange light pulses like a heartbeat. The bones of soldiers in royal green lie in drifts against the rocks. Then the mountain moves: a coil of grey-scaled serpent the width of a tower lifts from the scree, and a head crowned with horns of obsidian rises to look down at you. "More of the King's children?" it asks, in a voice like a rockslide. "No. You smell of a different crown."

**Features.**
- **The chute.** A 200-foot avalanche chute runs down the pass. Loud noise or area damage of 40 or more there triggers a rockslide (DC 40 Reflex, 10d10 bludgeoning, buried on a critical failure).
- **The magma vent.** A 30-foot-wide fissure of molten rock. Creatures that end a turn within 10 feet take 6d6 fire damage. Vashkra swims in it freely.
- **Soldiers' bones.** Fifty-two royal soldiers lie here. Searching them yields their regimental insignia and the King's original orders, both useful as evidence of royal aggression.
- **Narrow ledges.** The pass narrows to 15 feet in two places. Gargantuan Vashkra can't enter these without squeezing.

**Running the encounter.** Vashkra opens with conversation. She knows the PCs did not come from the King's city and is curious. Run an Influence encounter over 5 rounds. She has no patience for threats, and the curse makes any fight costly even if the PCs win.

**Vashkra the tor linnorm (Influence)**
- **Perception** DC 42
- **Discovery** DC 40 Nature or Arcana; DC 42 Society
- **Influence Skills** DC 42 Diplomacy, DC 44 Deception, DC 47 Intimidation, DC 40 Performance (she loves sagas of battle), DC 38 Lore about the river realms or the northern kingdom (news of the world)
- **Influence 2:** Vashkra agrees to let the PCs themselves pass.
- **Influence 4:** Vashkra names her tribute: a magic item of level 17 or higher, or an equivalent hoard (12,000 gp in metals and gems), delivered every year on midwinter. In return she lets caravans bearing the PCs' banner use the pass.
- **Influence 6:** Vashkra accepts a smaller tribute (6,000 gp per year) and promises to burn any royal or Wild Hunt force that enters the pass. She tells the PCs that "riders without tracks" have tested her pass twice this season.
- **Resistances** Mentions of the King or his city (+2 DC to all checks that round). Any threat to kill her (+5 DC Intimidation; she reminds them of the curse).
- **Weaknesses** Tales of great battles, especially the PCs' own war against the King (–2 DC Performance). Offering tribute up front (–2 DC Diplomacy).
- **Penalty** Two critical failures provoke her: she uses Pyroclastic Breath once as a warning and gives the PCs one more round to leave.

**Combat.** At party level 18 or higher, the PCs may choose to fight. Vashkra opens with Pyroclastic Breath, then uses Slashing Claws against multiple targets. She retreats into the magma vent to regenerate, which forces PCs to fight her near the fire. She keeps up Regeneration 20 until cold iron hits her. Whoever lands the killing blow must save against the Curse of Boiling Blood (DC 48 Will).

**Outcomes.**
- **Bargain.** The pass opens as a trade route. The kingdom gains a new Trade route between its western holdings and the mountains: +1 to Trade Agreements checks while the tribute is paid.
- **Vashkra slain.** The pass opens permanently. The killer carries the curse until a 9th-rank *cleanse affliction* or an equivalent ritual removes it.
- **PCs leave.** Vashkra remains. The PCs may return with tribute.

**Rewards.** 120 XP for any resolution at Influence 4 or more, or for a victory at party level 18. Treasure: the soldiers' gear yields 800 gp. Vashkra's hoard, reached through the magma vent (fire resistance or a DC 40 Athletics check to swim through it), holds 9,000 gp in obsidian, silver and gems, plus *major obsidian goggles*.

**Scaling.** At party level 19, add two magma elemental servants or raise the curse DC by 2. For a gentler table, Vashkra lets any party that reaches Influence 2 pass with no tribute.

### 19. Shadow on the Peaks

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 19: Western Mountains (a ruined dragon roost on a black-rock spire above a fen) |
| **Hex** | 4.1 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Exploration and sighting before Chapter 10; revenge ambush afterwards |
| **Threat** | Sighting: no combat. Ambush: Severe 120 at party level 18, Moderate 80 at party level 19. |
| **Creatures** | The black dragon (L21, Kingmaker) |

**The King's map note.** A tall black spire inked in, with a wing shape above it drawn again and again in heavy strokes as though he traced it while thinking. The note reads: "her old roost — empty? if so, mine."

**Background.** The black dragon once ruled these peaks. She now spends most of her time in her lair in the fey realm (Chapter 10, page 454). Every few months she flies over her old haunts. The King wanted her abandoned roost for its acid-etched treasures and sent scouts to watch. They saw her come back once and never set foot there again. The roost still holds a small cache and the fen below it remains poisoned by her Corrupt Water.

**Arrival (sighting).**
> A shadow sweeps across the fen, and the frogs fall silent. High above, a black dragon wheels around a spire of dark rock, her wings scattering mist, a long acid-burned scar visible along one flank. She circles once, twice, and then she climbs into the clouds and is gone.

**Arrival (revenge ambush).**
> The fen water hisses. Something huge slides under the surface toward you, and then the black dragon rises in a wave of black water, her scarred head lowered and her eyes fixed on you. "You entered my home," she says. "Now I enter yours."

**Features.**
- **The spire.** A 300-foot black-rock spire. Climbing it is a DC 38 Athletics check; flying is easier. The roost sits on a ledge near the top.
- **The roost.** An open cave strewn with bones and acid-scarred metal. Old royal survey stakes ring the entrance.
- **The fen.** Shallow acidic water (difficult terrain; creatures that end a turn submerged take 3d6 acid damage). The black dragon swims through it freely.
- **Corrupted pools.** Undrinkable water; plants here are dead.

**Running the encounter.**

*Before Chapter 10 (sighting).* The PCs glimpse the black dragon overhead. Let them track her with a DC 39 Survival check to the roost. The roost lies empty. Searching it reveals her old treasure cache and a fresh scale that a DC 39 Arcana check identifies as touched by fey magic. This foreshadows her lair in Chapter 10.

*After the PCs fight her in Chapter 10 (revenge ambush).* The black dragon hunts the PCs. She ambushes them when they cross the fen. She opens with Breath Weapon down the longest line of PCs, then uses Frightful Presence. She fights from the water, using Tail Lash against anyone who tries to Climb or Recall Knowledge, and flies off to the roost if reduced below 100 HP. If she retreats, the PCs can finish her at the roost.

**Outcomes.**
- **Sighting.** The PCs gain the roost cache and a clue about her lair in the fey realm (+2 circumstance bonus to Recall Knowledge about the black dragon in Chapter 10).
- **Ambush won.** The black dragon dies or flees for good. The fen begins to recover within a year.
- **Ambush lost or fled.** The black dragon keeps hunting the PCs and attacks again within a month.

**Rewards.** Sighting: 40 XP for finding the roost. Ambush: 120 XP at party level 18. Treasure (roost): 2,000 gp in tarnished coins and an acid-etched *+3 greater striking corrosive longsword*, corroded but intact. Treasure (ambush): her hoard remains in the fey realm, but her carried horn ornaments are worth 3,000 gp.

**Scaling.** At party level 19 or higher, add a black dragon drake or an acid-water hazard to the ambush. If the PCs never reach her den in the fey realm, keep the encounter as a sighting.

### 20. The Burning Giant

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 19: Western Mountains, burning toward the Uplands (a scorched valley with a dying village) |
| **Hex** | 2.4 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Combat with a kingdom-threat clock |
| **Threat** | Beyond Extreme at party level 16; hold this site. Base lerritan: Severe 120 at party level 18, Moderate 80 at party level 19. Elite lerritan (L22): Severe 120 at party level 19. |
| **Creatures** | Lerritan (L21, Bestiary 2); use the elite adjustment at party level 19 |

**The King's map note.** A line of hexes coloured in with red crayon, running from the Western Mountains toward the lowlands, with the note: "toward us — or toward them?" and an arrow pointing away from the King's city that has been scratched out and redrawn twice.

**Background.** A lerritan named Kaldurok woke under the Western Mountains and has followed a seam of obsidian down the valleys, burning everything it passes. It honours an evil elemental demigod of fire and wants to set a great fire in her name. The King's scouts tracked its path and found it heading toward the uplands. Each week it advances one hex. Unless stopped, it reaches one of the PCs' settlements within the season. Its current target is a herding village, Kettle Hollow, which lies directly in its path.

**Arrival.**
> The air shimmers with heat long before you see it. Black smoke climbs over the ridge, and then a giant the height of a watchtower steps into view, its skin glassy black, lava welling in the cracks of its chest. A small volcano on its back belches cinders. Below it, the roofs of a village smoke and crackle. "Run," the giant laughs, "or burn. My goddess welcomes both."

**Features.**
- **The burning village.** Kettle Hollow has 12 buildings. Each round the lerritan stays within 60 feet of the village, one building collapses (DC 38 Reflex for anyone inside, 8d6 fire and bludgeoning).
- **Villagers.** Thirty villagers, led by their headwoman Elder Maren, hide in a grain cellar and a well-house. Each round, a PC can Interact to lead up to 5 villagers to safety (the stream to the south is safe).
- **The stream.** A cold stream crosses the valley. The lerritan avoids water. Creatures in the stream gain fire resistance 10.
- **Obsidian scree.** Shards from the lerritan's skin make the ground around it difficult terrain within 20 feet.

**Running the encounter.** Run a 3-round evacuation clock alongside the fight. Villagers left at the end of round 3 take damage when the lerritan's Volcanic Eruption hits the village. The lerritan opens with Volcanic Eruption, then uses Tenacious Flames to stop the PCs recovering from persistent fire damage. It strikes with its warhammer and uses Throw Rock against anyone who flees to the stream. It fights to the death; it believes its demigod will reforge it.

**Outcomes.**
- **Lerritan slain, village saved.** Kettle Hollow survives. The PCs' kingdom gains 1 Fame and the villagers offer the PCs a home if they need one.
- **Lerritan slain, village lost.** The PCs end the threat. The kingdom suffers 1 Unrest from refugees.
- **PCs retreat.** The lerritan continues. One week later it reaches a PC settlement; run a Disaster kingdom event and reroll the encounter there.

**Rewards.** 120 XP at party level 18 (base) or 19 (elite). Treasure: the lerritan's obsidian warhammer is a *+3 greater striking flaming warhammer* sized for a Gargantuan creature (sell value or resize). Its glass skin yields 6,000 gp in volcanic glass, usable as a crafting reagent. The village elders give the PCs 500 gp in thanks.

**Scaling.** At party level 19, use the elite lerritan and add two fire elementals summoned by Volcanic Eruption. For a smaller party, give the villagers one more round to evacuate.

### 21. The Annihilator

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zone** | Zone 16: Uplands, on the Skyfall border (hill farms and a burning steading) |
| **Hex** | 0.6 on the borderlands map |
| **Type** | Combat + protect the survivors; linked to [#9 The Iron Juggernaut](#9-the-iron-juggernaut) |
| **Threat** | Moderate 80 XP at party level 16 |
| **Creatures** | 1 annihilator robot (level 18, custom conversion below) |

**The King's map note.** A line of small burned-house glyphs running west out of the Skyfall Wastes, each crossed through. At the end of the line: "no bodies — it takes them ALIVE. where?"

**Background.** An annihilator robot ranges out of the Skyfall Wastes on orders older than any living nation: burn structures, destroy growth, and collect survivors. It carries captives in four stasis bays and brings them to the docking cradle on the Iron Juggernaut's loop, where it transfers them into the Juggernaut's hold. Stasis keeps a captive alive only while they ride in the Annihilator. Once transferred, they starve. The Annihilator has burned six steadings along the border. The PCs arrive as it reaches the seventh, Corl's Steading, where a family of goatherds hides in the root cellar. Bay 1 already holds Wenna Corl, taken from the steading's pasture an hour ago.

**Arrival.**
> Smoke climbs from a hillside farm. In the yard stands a machine the size of a house on four clawed legs, a segmented tail curled over its back. Its shoulder guns swing across the barn, and the barn bursts into flame. A child screams from somewhere under the farmhouse.

**Features.**
- **Farmhouse.** Stone walls (standard cover). The root cellar beneath holds four Corls: Aldo, his mother Hesk, and two children. The Annihilator burns the farmhouse first.
- **Barn.** Burning from the first round. A creature that starts its turn inside takes 4d6 fire damage. The barn collapses at the end of round 4 (6d6 bludgeoning, DC 36 basic Reflex for anyone inside).
- **Goat pasture.** Open ground ringed by a dry-stone wall (standard cover, difficult terrain to cross).
- **Hillside track.** The road east toward the Skyfall Wastes and the Juggernaut's loop. The Annihilator leaves this way when its work is done.

**ANNIHILATOR ROBOT** — CREATURE 18

Rare, N, Gargantuan, Construct, Robot (Skyfall)

**Perception** +32; darkvision, low-light vision, tremorsense (imprecise) 60 feet

**Languages** Common (antique machine dialect)

**Skills** Acrobatics +30, Athletics +36, Intimidation +29

**Str** +9, **Dex** +3, **Con** +7, **Int** +2, **Wis** +2, **Cha** –5

---

**AC** 42; **Fort** +33, **Ref** +28, **Will** +27

**HP** 300; **Immunities** bleed, cold, death effects, disease, doomed, drained, fatigued, paralyzed, poison, sickened, unconscious; **Weaknesses** critical hits 15, electricity 15; **Resistances** fire 15

**Force Field** The annihilator projects a shimmering field with 60 Hit Points. Damage reduces the field before the robot's HP. At the start of each of its turns, the field regains 15 HP. If the field drops to 0, it collapses and can't recover for 1d4 rounds; then it returns at 15 HP.

**Suppressed Response** ↺ **Trigger** A creature within 120 feet critically hits the annihilator. **Effect** The annihilator makes a chain gun Strike against the triggering creature.

---

**Speed** 50 feet, climb 30 feet; booster jets

**Melee** ◆ claw +34 (reach 20 feet), **Damage** 3d12+16 slashing

**Ranged** ◆ chain gun +32 (deadly d12, range increment 200 feet), **Damage** 3d10+12 piercing

**Booster Jets** ◆ The annihilator gains a fly speed of 60 feet until the end of its next turn. It can't use Booster Jets again for 1 round.

**Combined Arms** ◆◆ The annihilator makes a claw Strike and a chain gun Strike, each at its current multiple attack penalty; the penalty increases after both.

**Plasma Lance** ◆◆ (electricity, fire) The tail projects a 120-foot line of plasma. Each creature in the line takes 9d6 fire and 9d6 electricity damage (DC 40 basic Reflex). The annihilator can't use Plasma Lance again for 1d4 rounds.

**Suppressing Fire** ◆◆ The chain guns sweep a 60-foot cone. Each creature in the area takes 6d12 piercing damage (DC 38 basic Reflex). A creature that fails is off-guard until the end of its next turn.

**Collect Specimen** ◆ The annihilator picks up an unconscious or dying creature within its reach and seals them in a stasis bay. The creature stops making recovery checks and its dying value can't increase while inside. Opening a bay takes two Interact actions and a DC 38 Thievery check, or destroying the annihilator. A bay holds one creature; the annihilator has four bays.

**Deliver Cargo** When two or more bays are full, or when the annihilator drops below 100 HP, it uses Booster Jets and leaves for the Juggernaut's docking cradle. It reaches the cradle in 8 hours and transfers every captive into the Juggernaut's hold, where stasis ends.

*Conversion notes.* Converted from the PF1e Annihilator Robot (CR 16). It sits at level 18 so it lands as a Moderate solo at party level 16. PF1e vulnerability to critical hits and electricity becomes the Zone 17 rule of weakness 15 to both. The PF1e force field (80 hp, fast healing 16) becomes Force Field. PF1e lore says annihilators carry survivors to unknown locations; Collect Specimen and Deliver Cargo send them to the Iron Juggernaut.

**Running the encounter.**
- **Burn first.** The annihilator follows its orders in sequence: structures, then growth, then survivors. It spends its first actions each round on the nearest standing structure (**Plasma Lance** through the farmhouse, chain guns on the barn) unless a PC deals it damage that round, in which case it turns on that PC.
- **The cellar.** The farmhouse falls after it takes 60 damage, exposing the root cellar. The Corls inside become targets for **Collect Specimen** once the robot knocks one unconscious. A PC can lead the family out through the cellar's back door in two rounds, or shelter them behind the pasture wall.
- **Against the PCs.** It opens with **Suppressing Fire** on clustered PCs, uses **Booster Jets** to reach ranged attackers, then **Combined Arms** each round. When a PC drops, it uses **Collect Specimen** at the first chance.
- **Bay 1.** Wenna Corl rides in bay 1. A PC adjacent to the flank hears her knocking. Opening her bay mid-fight frees her; she runs for the cellar.
- **Electricity.** Each electricity hit that deals 15 or more damage also stuns the Force Field: it doesn't regain HP at the start of the annihilator's next turn.

**Outcomes.**
- **Destroyed.** The bays open and the PCs recover everyone inside. A DC 38 Crafting check on its navigation core reveals the cradle's location and the Juggernaut's loop.
- **Escaped with captives.** **Deliver Cargo** carries the captives to the cradle. The PCs can follow its scorched trail (DC 34 Survival) and catch it at the cradle within 8 hours, or rescue the captives from the Juggernaut's hold before they starve. A captured PC has the same starvation clock as any other captive, 3 days before losing ground.
- **The family.** Count the Corls the PCs keep out of the bays. A family saved in full spreads word of the PCs' rescue through the border farms.

**Rewards.** 80 XP, plus 10 XP for each Corl kept free. Treasure: two chain guns that function as level 16 integrated firearms only while wired to a power source, a stasis-bay power cell worth 2,000 gp to skyfall buyers, and 1,500 gp in salvage plating. Kingdom benefit: the saved Corls and their neighbours settle in the PCs' nearest border settlement; reduce Unrest by 1.

**Scaling.** At party level 18, make it elite (+1 level). For a weaker party, reduce Force Field to 40 HP and remove Suppressed Response.
